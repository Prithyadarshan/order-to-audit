Order to Audit

One system that takes a small South Indian snack factory from a salesman's phone order to the auditor's monthly report. Every transaction is entered once, and it flows into stock, production, costing and accounts.

Two loops that meet at finished-goods stock

Most invoicing apps stop at the sales loop. The production loop, with recipe-based planned-versus-actual consumption, is what makes this problem interesting.

Sales loop

Salesman order (mobile)→Admin verifies stock→GST invoice (PDF)→Dispatch→Payments and outstanding

Production loop

Purchase→Issue per recipe (BOM)→Production run→Variance flags→Cost per packet
Both loops meet at finished-goods stock, and both feed the monthly reports.

What it does

Built in the order the brief asked for: must-haves first, then should-haves, then stretch goals.

Mobile order entry

Pick a customer, add products in packets or cartons, send. Orders taken offline are saved on the phone and sent when the network returns.

Stock check and GST invoice

Admin sees whether stock covers the order. CGST + SGST inside Tamil Nadu, IGST outside, numbered by financial year, as a PDF.

BOM production

Each run issues raw material against the recipe, compares planned with actual use and yield, and flags anything beyond 3%.

Ledger-based inventory

Balances are never typed in. Every movement is a row that points to its purchase, run, invoice or adjustment.

Costing

Weighted-average material cost plus labour, power and gas per run, divided by good packets, with margin against price.

Audit-ready reports

Sales register, GST summary shaped like GSTR-1 and 3B, stock ledger, production variance, ageing, audit trail.

Expiry-first dispatch

Batch numbers and expiry dates on every run. Dispatch takes the batches that expire first.

Extras

Tally XML export, mock e-invoice and e-way bill files, GPS visit log, Tamil screens for floor staff.

Design choices that keep the books honest

Stock is a ledger

An append-only table. Balance is always the sum of movements, and nothing can go negative.

No floats for money

Integer paise everywhere, with one rounding rule. Quantities are integer grams, millilitres and pieces.

Traceable and audited

Every movement links to its source. Edits are logged. Posted invoices are cancelled with a credit note, never deleted.

Tested

18 tests cover money, GST, stock, FEFO, credit notes and the whole scenario over HTTP, on SQLite and PostgreSQL 16.

Stack: Python, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, PostgreSQL, server-rendered pages. Full write-up in the design note.
